SHAFT Privacy Policy

For the SHAFT apps for iPhone, iPad, Apple Watch and Mac. Last updated 16 November 2026.

SHAFT is an open-source system that runs AI agents ("instances") on your own Mac. The SHAFT app is a client for the SHAFT server you run. The developer runs no service that your data passes through, and receives none of it.

What the developer collects

Nothing. The app has no accounts, no analytics, no advertising, no tracking and no third-party SDKs that collect data. The developer cannot see your messages, your instances, your files or your use of the app.

If you choose, in your device's settings, to share analytics with app developers, Apple may pass the developer anonymous crash reports and usage figures under Apple's privacy policy. You can turn this off at any time in Settings → Privacy & Security → Analytics & Improvements.

Where your data goes

The app sends what you write, say or share only to the SHAFT server you paired it with, on your own Mac, over your own private network (for example Tailscale). That includes:

  • your messages, their attachments, and what you send from the Share sheet;
  • your answers to an instance's questions, your comments on its documents, and your feedback on its replies;
  • in voice mode, the text of what you say (never the audio), and the list of voices installed on this device, so an instance can suggest one;
  • this device's platform (iPhone or iPad) when you pair it, and its push notification token, so your server can address notifications to it.

What your server then does with that content is up to you and how you set it up. Your instances use the AI model providers you choose on your Mac (for example OpenRouter or OpenAI), and those providers receive the content your instances send them, under their own terms and privacy policies. The developer is not a party to those exchanges.

What stays on this device

  • The pairing credential (your server's address and this device's access key) is kept in the Keychain, shared only with the app's own extensions. Unpairing deletes it here and revokes it on your server.
  • Drafts and unsent messages are kept in the app's own storage until they are sent or discarded.
  • Voice mode turns speech into text on the device with Apple's on-device speech recognition. The audio is never sent anywhere and is not kept. The microphone is used only while voice mode is open.
  • Read aloud uses the voices built into the device.
  • Your preferences (appearance, text size, voice settings) are kept on the device.

Apple Watch

The watch app holds no credential and talks to nothing on the network. It passes your question to the SHAFT app on your paired iPhone, which sends it to your server and returns the answer.

Updates of the Mac app

Only the official Developer ID download of the Mac app uses Sparkle to check for updates once a day. You can turn this off in Settings. The check fetches the update feed from GitHub Pages. Its User-Agent header carries the app's name and version, and GitHub sees your IP address. No system profile is sent. Builds you make yourself do not check for updates.

An update downloads from GitHub Pages as a DMG only when you click Install. Sparkle verifies its EdDSA signature before opening it. The developer receives no data from these checks or downloads. GitHub keeps its own logs under its privacy statement.

Notifications

When notifications are available, a push travels through Apple's Push Notification service and carries only a title: the instance's name and what happened. The message itself is fetched by the app from your own server when the notification arrives. Push notifications are not yet available for self-hosted servers.

The demo

"Try the demo" runs on sample data built into the app. Nothing you type in the demo leaves the device, and the demo is wiped when you leave it or close the app.

Children

The app is not directed at children and collects no data from anyone.

Your choices

Because all your data lives on your own Mac, you control it there: you can read or delete it with SHAFT on your Mac. Deleting the app removes its local data from this device; unpairing (Settings → Unpair) also revokes the device on your server.

Changes
